Assistant Professor of Subsurface Energy Systems in Geology
West Virginia University

WVU profile Google Scholar

I am an assistant professor in the Department of Geology and Geography at West Virginia University and the researcher behind GeoAIOrbit Lab. My experience spans subsurface energy systems, seismic interpretation, reservoir characterization, and scientific computing. I combine that experience with artificial intelligence to investigate geological and geophysical systems.

My research interests include subsurface energy systems, machine learning and computer vision for geoscience, geological carbon sequestration, geothermal resources, AI-assisted seismic interpretation, robotics and UAVs for exploration, and reproducible scientific software. The work emphasizes geologically meaningful, uncertainty-aware models rather than prediction without context.

Research approach

I connect physical understanding and interpretation experience with data-driven models. Current and emerging directions include automated fault detection, multimodal subsurface data fusion, simulation surrogates, carbon-storage monitoring, geothermal resource characterization, and computer-vision workflows for robotic and UAV observations. Expert interpretation and uncertainty analysis remain essential parts of every workflow.

Software

I have developed and co-developed several software including WheelerLab, FischerLab, and MinInversion for chronostratigraphic analysis, Fischer plots, and petrophysical inversion respectively. GeoAIOrbit Lab extends this software focus toward transparent, AI ready tools that make geoscience analysis easier to reproduce and evaluate.
